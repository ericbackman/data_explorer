## Best-supported transition window

Window: **2008-12 (Kobe IV first wear) to 2020-21**, with the pivot at 2008-12 and the majority crossing somewhere between 2018 and 2020-21. Basis:
- Start: the Kobe IV on-court debut date and Kobe's stated reasons (rows above, ESPN 2008-12-11).
- Midpoint: a team podiatrist put lows at "roughly half" in March 2018 [AIP, 2018-03-16]. This is an estimate, and it is the only mid-period figure I could open.
- End: a measured 61% low, 33% mid, 6% high in 2020-21 [Tansey 2023].
- There is **no measured share for any season before 2020-21**, and no year-by-year series. The "pivot at 2008-09" is a design and marketing date, not a measured adoption date. The 2010 WSJ and NPD figures cover the consumer market, so they show a direction (high-top retail share 20% to 8%) and cannot be used as NBA shares.

## Synthesis

Chuck Taylor and Air Jordan 1 anchors are 1921 (unopened) and 1985 [Nike page]. The Pump launched 1989-11-24 and Brown's 1991-02-09 dunk-contest win followed. Lows existed earlier, for example the Jet Flight in 2001 [Complex 2014], so Kobe IV was not the first low. It was the first widely adopted one by a star. Kobe wore it from about 2008-12-19, asking for a "true, genuine low-top" [T1], while a team podiatrist warned of more ankle injuries [T2]. By 2010 the consumer retail share of lows had risen from about 11% to 29% [Marketplace]. By 2018-03 a team podiatrist put NBA lows at about half [T4]. In 2020-21 the measured figure was 61% low, 33% mid, 6% high [S5].

On injury: the one randomised trial (622 players) found no significant difference [S1]. A 2001 observational study found air cells, not collar height, as the shoe-related risk [S2]. Cochrane 2001 said high-top benefit was not established [S3]. A 2020 meta-analysis summary found neither height superior [S4]. The only NBA data (2020-21) found no difference in injury rate or days missed by height [S5]. Lab work shows collar height changes muscle timing and joint motion, with mixed results [S6, S7]. The NBA changed its colour rule in 2018 for expression, not safety [R1, R2], and I found no height rule.

## Conflicts and gaps

- **NBA share of highs, 2010.** Newser says 80% (late 1980s) to 8% of NBA players. Marketplace's account of the same WSJ piece says 20% to 8% of the shoe market, 8 years apart. Trust Marketplace (it quotes the author), but neither is the WSJ original. Do not chart the 80% figure.
- **Cochrane status.** Europe PMC record shows no withdrawal; a search summary said the 2001 version was withdrawn and replaced by an update (2011 link in the record). I did not open Cochrane Library. Cite it as "2001 version".
- **Kobe IV dates.** ESPN (2008-12-11) said on-court debut "expected" 2008-12-19 and US stores in February 2009. I did not open a page confirming actual debut or retail date. Complex review date is page metadata, not verified against the article.
- **Kyrie 1 is not a low, and the premise "signature shoe is a low by brand and year" is not supported by what I opened.** I only have Curry One Low (2015-07-17) and Kyrie Low 1 (2018-07-15) from unopened search results, and nothing on KD or Harden height.
- **No per-season share series exists in what I found.** A definitive answer needs a per-game shoe tally (Kix Stats, which compiles NBA shoe wear, sits behind the Sneaker Freaker 2021 article, which gives counts for Kobe 5, 6 and KD 13 but no height share) or the Tansey method repeated for other seasons.
- S5 does not give injury counts by height, so rates by height cannot be computed from what I read. Full text is on PMC10748690.
