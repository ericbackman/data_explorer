## Question
Outside evidence for a public investigation of NBA injury rates by era. As-of 2026-11-05. A: peer-reviewed injury epidemiology over time, schedule/load literature, NBA statements on Achilles injuries. B: total-minutes comparison, NBA Finals starters vs NCAA, EuroLeague, WNBA. C: league pace history, the possessions formula, any pace-injury study. Excluded: Wikipedia and its mirrors, listicles, forums. Pages that cookie-walled or returned 429 are listed at the end. Journal abstracts were read through the Europe PMC REST record (EPMC) because pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov returned cookie walls; full texts were not read. Numbers below come from abstracts only.

## Synthesis
**A. What the epidemiology says.** The two NBA-wide datasets of the 1988-2005 era are A1 and A2: one database of 1,094 players and 3,843 player-seasons covers 1988-89 to 1997-98 (A2) and the 17-year paper extends the same project (A1). Ankle sprains were the most frequent injury and patellofemoral inflammation cost the most games in both (A1, A2). Starkey put the game-related injury rate at about twice the college rate (A2). Neither abstract gives a per-exposure NBA rate, so a clean 1990s-to-2020s comparison needs a new calculation from public data, not a published number. The modern league-run database (A3) reports 5.6-7.0 game-loss injuries per 10,000 player-minutes for 2014-15 to 2018-19, with no significant trend (P = 0.19) and a lower playoff rate of 2.8 (A3). Anything claiming an era-over-era rise must say that those two datasets use different definitions (A1, A2 reportable-injury definition vs A3 game-loss).

**Achilles.** Silver counted seven in 2024-25 (4 regular season, 3 playoffs), zero the year before, and four as the previous high (A12). Journal tallies depend on inclusion rules: 22 players 2000-01 to 2024-25 (A7), 19 ruptures 2014-15 to 2024-25 (A8), 60 players 1958-2024 (A9), 12 players 2010-2025 after a return-to-play filter (A10). Two of these papers call 2024-25 a record (A8, A10). One finds ruptures at higher minutes in the 10-15 games before injury (A8); another finds a prior calf strain linked to rupture with OR 2.49 (A9). Silver said the league had an expert panel and was using AI video review, and he doubted game count was the cause (A12). I found no separate formal "Achilles study" announcement page beyond that interview.

**Schedule and load.** Lewis found injury odds rise with minutes (+2.87% per 96 minutes) and fall with rest (-15.96% per day) over 2012-2015 (A4). Teramoto, same seasons, found back-to-backs alone not associated with injury but away games were (A5). Herzog found that games missed for rest rose 125% over 2014-15 to 2022-23 and that resting was not tied to lower later injury risk (A6). Kerr's pace-and-schedule explanation is opinion (A14), and the same article says total injuries are not up, only stars' missed games.

**B. Minutes.** I found no sourced published comparison; the "2-3x" figure is my own calculation here. Team ceilings (arithmetic, no overtime): NBA 82 x 48 = 3,936 regular-season minutes, plus up to 28 playoff games x 48 = 1,344, total 5,280 (B1 for 82; the 28-game maximum is 4 rounds x 7 games and was not sourced). EuroLeague 2024-25: 34 x 40 = 1,360 regular season (B4); 2025-26 format is 38 games (search result of the official page, unopened). WNBA 2025: 44 x 40 = 1,760 (B5, B8). NCAA: 31 x 40 = 1,240 (B7; 40-minute length unsourced), before tournament games. Actual stars, Finals teams: Gilgeous-Alexander 2,598 regular-season minutes + 851 playoff = 3,449 in 99 games (B1, B2); Siakam 2,548 + 771 = 3,319 (B1, B2). Ratios, my arithmetic: 2,598 / 1,508 (Plum, WNBA regular season) = 1.72; 3,449 / 1,508 = 2.29 (playoff minutes counted for the NBA only). 2,598 / 1,059 (Hayes, likely EuroLeague regular-season leader) = 2.45; 3,449 / 1,233 (Nunn, EuroLeague incl. playoffs) = 2.80. So "2-3x" holds against EuroLeague, and for the WNBA only when playoffs are included on the NBA side. NCAA star minutes were not found, so no NCAA ratio is given. All the star comparisons pick one leader per league and are not like-for-like on playoffs.

**C. Pace.** BBR league averages: 126.1 (1959-60), 127.7 (1960-61), 126.2 (1961-62) (C1); 107.8 (1973-74), 100.6 (1988-89) (C2); trough 88.9 in the 50-game 1998-99 lockout season and 90.1 in 2003-04 (C1, C2); 92.0 (2012-13), 96.4 (2016-17), 100.3 (2019-20), 98.5 (2023-24), 98.8 (2024-25), 99.4 (2025-26) (C2). Formula in C3. The one pace-vs-injury test found, on Achilles ruptures, shows no significant correlation (C4), and Kerr's pace claim is opinion (A14).

## Conflicts and gaps
- **Achilles counts differ** (22, 19, 60, 12; A7-A10) because of date ranges and inclusion rules; Silver's seven (A12) matches the papers' "record" in 2024-25 (A8, A10). Trust Silver for his own tally (league source), and each paper only within its stated window.
- **Pace 2025-26:** BBR season value 99.4 (C2) vs Kerr's median 101.5 early in the season (C5). Different measures (full-season league average vs median of teams after ~12 games); not a conflict, but do not mix them. Trust BBR for the season figure.
- **EuroLeague minutes leader:** 1,233 (Nunn, includes playoffs) vs 1,059 (Hayes, likely regular season) from different BBR pages (B4). Need the official EuroLeague regular-season stats page.
- **1960s pace:** BBR's glossary says Pace starts 1973-74 while season pages show 1959-62 values (C1, C3); the method for pre-1973 values is undocumented.
- A definitive era-over-era injury-rate comparison needs: raw NBA injury records with a consistent definition across 1988-2025 (not published in a paper I found), or a documented public-data build (transaction/injury-report data).
- A definitive minutes comparison needs: official EuroLeague and NCAA season totals for a starter, and sourced game lengths.
