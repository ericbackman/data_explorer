NBA · injuries · 1976-2026

The Injury Era

Rotation players miss more games than they ever have. The league's own box scores say by how much and since when; its injury reports say where it hurts. Pace, minutes, the schedule and shoe height each get measured against that line, and most of the popular explanations do not survive the measuring.

Chapter 1 · The burden

Games missed by the players who play

A box score names who played. A player who appears on two dates and is absent for the team's games in between missed those games, whatever the reason. Count that for every player who averages 20 minutes a night (at least 10 games), and divide by team-games, and you have one measure of lost availability that means the same thing in 1977 and 2026. It needs no injury report, no listing rule, and no reporter.

Chapter 2 · The reasons

What the box score says about why

From 1996-97 every nba.com box score carries a comment for each listed player who did not play: a sprained left ankle, a coach's decision, a suspension. Two things changed in how the league writes them. Through 2002-03 the whole roster is listed, about eighteen names a team; from 2003-04 only the active roster, so a player on the inactive list simply vanishes. And from 2017-18 the body part is gone: the comment reads "Injury/Illness" and nothing more. So this chapter sizes the reasons; it is not a rate.

Chapter 3 · Where it hurts

Injury location by era

Three sources name the body part, each for its own stretch of years: the box-score comments through 2016-17, the injured-list moves that Pro Sports Transactions logged for 2010 to 2019 (through files other people published), and the league's official injury report from December 2018, which lists every player and the reason before every game. Below the knee means ankle, foot, Achilles, calf and shin.

Chapter 4 · Pace

Possessions per 48 minutes

Pace is estimated from each team's shots, rebounds, turnovers and free throws with Basketball-Reference's formula, which this build reproduces to within 0.04 possessions on every season checked. Turnovers were not recorded for every game until 1985-86, so the computed line starts there; the earlier dots are Basketball-Reference's published league averages.

Chapter 5 · Minutes and the schedule

Who plays how much

The 82-game season has not changed since 1967-68. What changed is how the minutes inside it are spread. The top eight players on each team play fewer minutes per game than they did, the 3,000-minute season has nearly disappeared, and the schedule itself has fewer back-to-backs than at any point in the data.

Against the other leagues

A common claim, with no published source found for it, is that an NBA Finals team's players log two to three times the minutes of players in college, EuroLeague or the WNBA. The ceilings come from each league's schedule length; the stars are the 2024-25 minutes leaders of the two Finals teams against each other league's minutes leader.
